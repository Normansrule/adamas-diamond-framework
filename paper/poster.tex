\block{}{\Large Diamond has the largest breakdown field and thermal conductivity of any practical semiconductor, and a defect, the nitrogen-vacancy (NV) center, that is a qubit at room temperature. ADAMAS follows one path from wafer to quantum processor with executable, cited models: \hl{\NumRefs{} references}, \hl{\NumFigures{} regenerable figures}, a process traveler, a placed processor, circuit-level error correction, and experiments from US\$100. \hl{Every number on this poster is generated by the package.}}
\begin{columns}
\column{0.5}
\block{1 · Power: diamond wins when hot}{
\begin{tikzfigure}\includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{fig03_ron_vs_bv.png}\end{tikzfigure}
Measured lateral hydrogen-terminated diamond MOSFETs reach \BestBV\,V and sit \MeasuredOverSi$\times$ below silicon's vertical limit. Only \FionDriftPct\% of the boron in a bulk-doped 1.2\,kV drift is ionized at 300\,K, so in an 800\,V electric-vehicle inverter a bulk-doped diamond switch loses \LossDiamond\,W against \LossSiC\,W for silicon carbide (ratio \PowerRatioRoom), and wins when hot: \hl{\PowerRatioHot$\times$} at a 523\,K junction (median \UncPowerHotPfifty$\times$ with material uncertainty). Sobol indices rank what to measure first: diamond hole mobility, by a Hall measurement costing a few thousand dollars.
\begin{tikzfigure}\includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{fig49_uncertainty.png}\end{tikzfigure}}
\block{2 · How to build it}{
\begin{tikzfigure}\includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{fig41_process_flow.png}\end{tikzfigure}
A 20-step traveler: hydrogen termination makes the channel, gold protects it, one oxygen plasma isolates devices, and all heat is spent before the first metal. Wafers that already hold NV centers need the low-temperature ($\le$500\,$^\circ$C) hydrogen option, because a hot hydrogen plasma passivates them. The PDK-0 kit places the 4-bit DIA-4 processor (\DiaCells{} cells) in about 1\,mm$^2$.

\block{3 · Two-qubit gates: an open discrepancy}{
\begin{tikzfigure}\includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{fig36_published_gate_check.png}\end{tikzfigure}
With the published 25\,nm pair, coherence alone allows a Bell fidelity of \hl{\FcohLow{} to \FcohHigh}, depending on the noise model. The measured 0.67 (charge-preselected) lies below that whole range: a known discrepancy. The earlier preparation explanation is withdrawn after the audit.}
\block{4 · Error correction with slow readout}{
\begin{tikzfigure}\includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{fig40_biased_noise_xzzx.png}\end{tikzfigure}
Circuit-level simulations (Stim, PyMatching): scaling stops near \BreakevenStdMs\,ms readout for a 1\,s nuclear memory; the bias-tailored XZZX code extends this to about \hl{\BreakevenXZZXMs\,ms}. With two-level qubits only CZ keeps a bias (CNOT at most \EtaCnot): native XZZX circuits then reach a threshold of \hl{\ThrXZZXBias\%} at bias 100, against \ThrCSSBias\% for the standard code. RSA-2048, calibrated to the published 8-hour estimate, fits on a \RSADieMm\,mm die but runs \hl{\RSAYears{} years} at 1\,ms readout (P10 to P90: \UncQPten{} to \UncQPninety), assuming a 25\,$\mu$s, 0.3\% inter-cell link that does not exist yet. With biased gate noise the standard code needs \RSAQubitsMCSSBiased\,M qubits; XZZX needs \hl{\RSAQubitsMXZZX\,M}.}
