Silicon's cousin,
built for extremes.

ADAMAS asks what it takes to build chips on diamond wafers instead of silicon: kilovolt power switches, logic that runs at 400 °C, and qubits that work at room temperature. Every number here comes from a cited equation or measurement, and every lab runs the same physics as the Python package.

Head to head

Same crystal pattern. Very different numbers.

Silicon and diamond share the "diamond cubic" lattice. Diamond's atoms are smaller and bonded far more tightly, which changes almost everything. Pick a property.

Try it

Paint with heat. Watch it escape.

Move your cursor across the slab. The left half is silicon, the right half diamond: same grid, same time step, same heat. Diamond's heat spreads about 13 times faster, which is why a diamond chip runs cool.

Silicon · α = 0.91 cm²/s
Diamond · α = 12.3 cm²/s

Thermal diffusivity α = κ/(ρc). Sources: [glassbrenner1964] [wei1993]. The full four-material simulation is in the Heat Race.

Honest obstacles

What is still hard

Doping

Deep dopants

Under 1% of boron atoms are active at room temperature, against 90% in silicon.

Wafers

76 mm, not 300

Reproducible 3-inch single-crystal wafers arrived in 2026. Silicon has used 300 mm for decades.

Logic

No easy CMOS

The first n-channel diamond MOSFET is from 2024, so logic is still p-channel only and burns static power.

Qubits

Placement and charge

Two qubits must sit within about 25 nm, and each is in the right charge state only about 75% of the time.
