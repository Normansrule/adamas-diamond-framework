Power Lab

A three-phase inverter, like the one between an electric car's battery and its motor. Pick the application, the switch material, and how hot it runs. The die size is chosen for minimum loss.

One phase leg switching (slowed down about a million times)
Electric cars flip their power switches on and off thousands of times a second. Every flip wastes a little energy as heat, and so does the switch's resistance while it is on. A better material wastes less, so the car goes farther and needs a smaller radiator. Diamond is also the only one that gets better when it is hot.
Per switch, P = I²Ron,sp/A + ½Coss,spAV²f. Choosing the die area A to minimize P gives P = 2IV√(fRon,spCoss,sp/2): conduction and switching losses come out equal, and the material's merit is √(R·C). The required heat-sink thermal resistance is (Tj,max − Tambient)/P.
Inputs come from adamas.converter via data/site.json and are tested to agree with the Python within 1%. Ron(T) includes lattice-mobility exponents and, for bulk-doped diamond, the rise of boron ionization with temperature; the measured diamond device (a lateral hole-gas MOSFET) has no ionization gain. Gate charge, reverse recovery, and GaN dynamic Ron are not modeled; ceilings are package and reliability limits (chapter E9).
