Gate Bias Lab

Room-temperature spins mostly lose phase (Z errors) rather than flipping (X errors). The XZZX code turns that bias into a higher threshold, but only if the two-qubit gates keep it. Drop an error into a gate and follow it out.

Our qubits usually make one kind of mistake, which is good news: a clever code can watch for that kind. But mistakes travel through the gates. One gate leaves the mistake as it is. The other gate turns it into the other kind of mistake on its way out, and the clever code is fooled.
A CZ gate is diagonal, so a Z error commutes with it and leaves as a Z. Two-level hardware usually makes a CNOT from a CZ between two Hadamards on the target. A Hadamard swaps X and Z, so a phase error during the CZ comes out of the CNOT as a bit flip on the target, and an X error before the CZ also drags a Z onto the control. The bias of the target's errors is inverted: Z and X trade places while Y stays put, so η = pZ/(pX + pY) becomes 1/(2η + 1) when the Hadamards are short; at this lab's default Hadamard length (0.002 of the CZ time) the model gives 0.33, 0.05, and 0.007 for η = 1, 10, and 100.
Errors are propagated to the gate's output by Clifford conjugation (H: X↔Z; CZ: X_a → X_a Z_b; checked against Stim's PauliString.after in the tests). Each segment gets independent Pauli errors with probability proportional to its duration and bias η, placed at the end of the segment; the bars are first order in p. Because of that placement, an X error partway through the CZ, which would also put a Z on the other qubit, is not counted, so the two-qubit (spread) fraction is a lower bound. This is a teaching model of one construction. A bias-preserving CNOT needs a continuous symmetry two-level spins lack (it exists for cat qubits [guillaud2019] [puri2020]); careful two-level designs keep a residual bias of about 5 [etxezarreta2025]. That is why chapter E6.1e gives CZ the full bias and CNOTs at most 5, and why XZZX reaches 1.8% instead of the 3.3% of the bias-preserving upper bound.
