Entanglement Lab

Alice and Bob share entangled pairs and no wire. Can Alice send Bob a message through the entanglement alone? Try, then see what the pairs are really good for.

Imagine two magic coins that always land the same way, even far apart. When Bob flips his, he just sees heads or tails at random, half and half. Alice cannot choose how her coin lands, so she cannot make Bob's coin say anything. Only when they call each other and compare lists do they see the coins matched. The phone call is the message; the coins just make the call more useful.
For a shared pair v|Φ⁺⟩⟨Φ⁺| + (1 − v)I/4 measured along angles a and b, the joint probability is P(k, l) = (1 + (−1)^(k+l) v cos(a − b))/4. Summing over Alice's outcome k gives 1/2 for each of Bob's outcomes, whatever a is: Bob's statistics carry no trace of Alice's choice (no-communication theorem). Teleportation uses the pair plus two classical bits to move an unknown qubit with fidelity (1 + v)/2; without the bits Bob holds I/2. Doing without entanglement (measure, phone, re-prepare) gives 2/3 on average, so a pair helps only if v > 1/3. The Bell (CHSH, Clauser–Horne–Shimony–Holt) value S = 2√2 v exceeds the local-hidden-variable bound 2 when v > 1/√2.
The browser uses the closed forms above; adamas.entangle computes the same quantities with full density matrices and the node tests compare them at fixed inputs. no_signaling_check applies random measurements and unitaries to random two-qubit states and finds Bob's reduced state unchanged to round-off. NV-to-NV entanglement, Bell violation and teleportation are demonstrated only at cryogenic temperature, with photons carrying the entanglement [bernien2013] [hensen2015] [pompili2021] [hermans2022]. Storing pre-shared entanglement in room-temperature ¹³C memories [maurer2012] is proposed, not shown. Not modelled: photon loss, heralding rates, memory decoherence during the classical delay.
